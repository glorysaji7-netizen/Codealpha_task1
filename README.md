# Unemployment Analysis in India

## Project Overview

For this project, I explored unemployment trends in India using Python. The main aim was to understand how unemployment rates changed over time and how they differed between Indian states and rural and urban areas.

I used data analysis and visualisation techniques to explore the dataset, identify patterns and understand some of the major changes in unemployment during the period covered by the data.

This project helped me improve my practical experience with Python, data cleaning, exploratory data analysis and creating visualisations.

## Objectives

The main objectives of this project were:

- Understand the unemployment situation across different Indian states.
- Compare unemployment rates between rural and urban areas.
- Analyse how unemployment changed from month to month.
- Explore the possible impact of the COVID-19 period on unemployment.
- Understand labour force participation rates across different areas.
- Present the findings using clear and meaningful graphs.

## Tools and Technologies Used

The following tools and Python libraries were used:

- **Python** – Main programming language.
- **Pandas** – For loading, cleaning and analysing the dataset.
- **NumPy** – For numerical operations.
- **Matplotlib** – For creating graphs and visualisations.
- **Seaborn** – Imported for data visualisation.

## Dataset Information

The project uses the Unemployment in India dataset.

The dataset contains information about:

- Region
- Date
- Frequency
- Estimated Unemployment Rate (%)
- Estimated Employed
- Estimated Labour Participation Rate (%)
- Area (Rural or Urban)

The original dataset contained 754 rows and 7 columns.

During the data-cleaning process, I identified 14 completely empty rows and removed them. The cleaned dataset contained 740 rows with no missing values.

## Project Steps

### 1. Importing Libraries and Loading Data

I started by importing the required Python libraries and loading the unemployment dataset using Pandas.

I also explored the first few rows to understand how the data was organised.

### 2. Data Cleaning

Before starting the analysis, I checked the dataset for missing values and duplicate records.

I found 14 completely empty rows and removed them. After cleaning, the dataset contained 740 records with no missing values or duplicate rows.

### 3. Exploring Unemployment Across States

I calculated the average unemployment rate for each Indian state.

I then created a bar chart to compare the unemployment levels across different regions.

Tripura had the highest average unemployment rate in the dataset, followed by Haryana and Jharkhand.

### 4. Monthly Unemployment Analysis

I converted the date column into a proper date format and analysed the average unemployment rate month by month.

I created a line chart to understand how unemployment changed between May 2019 and June 2020.

The analysis showed a significant increase in unemployment during April and May 2020.

### 5. Understanding the COVID-19 Period

I explored the unemployment trend around the COVID-19 period.

The graph showed a noticeable increase beginning in April 2020, following the period marked as the beginning of COVID-19 in March 2020.

This helped highlight how unemployment levels changed during this period.

### 6. Rural vs Urban Unemployment

I compared the average unemployment rates between rural and urban areas.

The results were:

- Rural unemployment: 10.32%
- Urban unemployment: 13.17%

The analysis showed that the average unemployment rate was higher in urban areas than in rural areas.

### 7. Labour Force Participation Analysis

I also explored the estimated labour force participation rate.

The overall average participation rate was approximately 42.63%.

The rural and urban comparison showed:

- Rural participation rate: 44.46%
- Urban participation rate: 40.90%

This showed a higher average labour force participation rate in rural areas in the dataset.

## Key Findings

After completing the analysis, I identified the following findings:

1. Tripura recorded the highest average unemployment rate among the regions included in the dataset.
2. Urban areas had a higher average unemployment rate than rural areas.
3. Unemployment increased significantly during April and May 2020.
4. May 2020 recorded the highest monthly average unemployment rate in the period analysed, at approximately 24.88%.
5. Rural areas had a higher average labour force participation rate than urban areas.
6. The overall average unemployment rate across the cleaned dataset was approximately 11.79%.
